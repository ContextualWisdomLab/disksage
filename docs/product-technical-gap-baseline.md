## Product invariants

1. Scan and classification are read-only until a separately authorized reclaim boundary is reached. Logical size, allocated size, filesystem identity, active use, materialization, provider state, ontology labels, and model advice are distinct evidence.
2. User-file removal is reversible and journaled. Permanent general user-file deletion is not a supported DiskSage action.
3. Regenerable-cache policy never grants irreversible authority merely from names, signatures, byte counts, or approval phrases. `--purge-proven-cache-trash` remains inspection-only and shipped execution fails closed when identity-bound permanent-delete capability is unavailable.
4. A successful mutation must consume the exact reviewed object. Repeating pathname checks is not sufficient authority for replacement or irreversible deletion. Unsupported platform primitives fail before mutation rather than falling back to delete-and-create or recursive pathname deletion.
5. Cloud copy, provider sync proof, local presence, local eviction, and source removal are separate transitions. Stale/incomplete provider evidence, quota/auth/headroom ambiguity, collisions, active use, placeholders, or identity gaps fail closed.
6. Models and ontology may classify, explain, or recommend but cannot override deterministic filesystem/deletion evidence or mutate DiskSage truth. Model-backed repository workflows consume `orchestrator/free` through contextual-orchestrator and do not pin a paid/direct provider locally.
7. Settings, receipts, approvals, and recovery records distinguish first-run absence from an existing unreadable or unsafe document. Failed replacement must preserve the previous complete value.
8. Crash/power-loss and durability claims are platform-specific and must name the primitive actually exercised. macOS `fsync` evidence is not described as stronger `F_FULLFSYNC` evidence.
9. Shared workflow behavior has one canonical owner. Source-absent conditional success is admission evidence, not runtime proof.
10. Release readiness belongs only to the exact protected lineage after all applicable deterministic, security, model/review, provenance, reproducibility, rollback, and human-governance gates pass. Predecessor success never transfers automatically.

## Standards and owner traceability

- POSIX pathname operations such as `rename()/renameat()` do not by themselves prove that the final mutation consumed the exact previously reviewed source object. Linux-only primitives remain platform-scoped rather than treated as portable authority.
- Windows same-object publication/replacement work remains tied to native handle-oriented semantics such as `FILE_RENAME_INFO`/`SetFileInformationByHandle`, with explicit reparse, ACL, and durability evidence required before capability is exposed.
- macOS persistence claims distinguish ordinary `fsync` evidence from stronger `F_FULLFSYNC` semantics.
- GitHub Actions evidence is exact-head evidence only when the relevant source exists and the workflow actually executes it. Failed-job rerun/carry-forward records must not be treated as proof that every reported job physically executed on the same attempt.
- Issue #170 owns broader deletion/recovery safety; issue #342 owns private-publication/exact-source replacement; issue #340 owns TopFiles browser acceptance. Central CodeQL/model workflow defects remain canonical `.github` owner work rather than DiskSage leaf substitutes.

No statement in this baseline grants irreversible deletion, cross-service SQL, mutable sibling dependency, source-copy authority, direct provider/model override, self-approval, or release-gate bypass. Live exact refs and released owner contracts supersede this snapshot whenever they advance.
